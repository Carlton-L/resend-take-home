Transfers — design reference

The screens for moving a held name from one account to another, using the app's own tokens. This is a reference for docs/TRANSFERS.md, not a build. Nothing here ships; the state and the fields exist, the writer does not. The flow drawn below is the leading direction in the spec rather than a settled decision, and who decides at the close of a contest is still open. The two directions it was weighed against are listed first.

Designed · not built · leading direction

Directions considered

Three ways to resolve a second account proving control. The screens after this draw the third; the other two are in the spec's decisions.

DirectionWhat it doesWhy not, or why
Instant moveProving control moves the name at once.Matches that control moved, and gives the holder no window to answer before the name leaves.
Holder approvesThe name moves only when the holder says yes.Reads as safe, and deadlocks a sold or abandoned name on an account that will never answer.
Notice period (drawn here)Proving control opens a contest; the holder is alerted and has a window to reassert; silence moves the name.Leading direction: it alerts the holder and still resolves when they are gone. Who decides at the window's close stays open.

The screens below are a sketch of the notice-period direction, not a commitment to build it. If the decision lands on instant move or holder-approval instead, the alert and the email carry over and the reassert step is what changes.

1 · The challenger, during a contest

A second account proved control of a name someone else holds. The proof opens a contest rather than handing the name over.

Held · decision due
loresprite.comYou proved control. The account that holds this name has until the date below to answer.
✓ 5 of 5 answered · your record is live at _domainclaim-challenge.loresprite.com
Decision due 20 September 2026 at 14:02 UTC. If the holder does not reassert control by then, the name moves to you.

What it says and does not say. The name is not called theirs while the window is open, because it is not. The screen shows the proof landed and states the date the contest closes. The account being challenged is never named on either side.

2 · The incumbent's list, with the notice

A persistent notice, in the attention tone, that stays until the contest ends. It cannot be dismissed.

Someone proved control of loresprite.com and has requested it. If this was not you, reassert control by 20 September 2026, 14:02 UTC or the name will move.
loresprite.comContestedsince 16 September 2026 at 14:02 UTC···
carlton.devVerified···
futurity.scienceVerified···

What changed. The held row moves verified → contested and carries the date the window closes. Contested is a holding state, so the name stays reserved to this account until the contest ends. The notice repeats the record screen's one action so a long list cannot bury it.

3 · The email that decides

The channel that survives losing the zone. An incumbent who never opens the app, or who has lost DNS control, is still reached here.

From  DomainClaim <notify@carlton.dev>To     you@loresprite.comSubj  Someone proved control of loresprite.com
DomainClaim

Control of loresprite.com was proved from another account

You currently hold loresprite.com. A short time ago, another account added a valid record to its DNS and proved control of the same name.

If you sold or handed off this domain, you can ignore this, and the name will move on 20 September 2026, 14:02 UTC.

If this was not you, reassert control before that time to keep the name. You keep it by confirming your own record still answers.

Review this claim

You are receiving this because your account holds this domain. This is the one time the app will email you out of turn about it.

Why email carries it. The in-app notice reaches an incumbent who opens the app; the email reaches one who does not, and survives them losing the zone, which the app session does not. It gives the date and the action and does not name the challenging account. Same sender and path as the status-change email in the main RFC.

4 · The incumbent reasserts, or approves

Two ways to end the contest from the holder's side. Doing nothing is the third, and it moves the name when the window closes.

Contested
loresprite.comAnother account proved control. You hold the name until 20 September 2026, 14:02 UTC.
Reasserting checks that your own record still answers. If it does, the name stays with you and the contest closes. If your record is gone, you no longer control this zone, and reasserting cannot bring the name back.

What each does. Reassert runs the same proof the claim was built on: an incumbent who still controls the zone holds the name by doing nothing to their DNS, one who has lost it cannot, which is the correct outcome. Approve is the handover made explicit, for a seller who wants it done rather than waited out.

5 · The lifecycle

Where the contest sits in a held name's states, and the three ways out.

verified — challenger proves control → contested
from contested: verifiedreassert, or record still answers
  revokedapprove, or window closes → name moves to challenger (→ verified)
Ends byIncumbent's zoneOutcome
reassertstill controlledstays with the incumbent, challenge declined
approvehanded offmoves to the challenger at once
window closeslost, or abandonedmoves to the challenger, no deadlock

The move is one statement in a transaction: the incumbent's row leaves the holding set for revoked, the challenger's pending row enters it as verified, so the unique index is never violated mid-move. Closing the window needs the same scheduled job the grace window needs, which is why neither is built.
